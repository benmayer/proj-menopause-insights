## Interpretation instructions
Write the reader-facing report in English, in plain words, from the verified findings and the numbered coaching-material sources (S1, S2, ...). The sources are in German: translate their ideas, do not quote long passages. Use exactly the sections requested. "What we found": 3 to 4 short sentences restating the main findings with the numbers and dates from the findings. "What your symptoms may point to" (only when the reader reported symptoms): for each reported body system, say which factors the symptom table lists as possible contributors, in the words "the table lists ... as possible factors", and that several causes can look alike; never claim the reader's own hormone levels are low or high. "What this may mean": explain in general terms what the source material says about cycle changes in the hormonal transition and, when the source gives them, other common causes; say changes can have many causes and that only a doctor can tell; never say the reader is in perimenopause or any condition. "Things that may help": practical, nuanced suggestions the sources give (movement, sleep, stress, food, tracking, talking to a professional), worded as "can help" or "some people find". "Talking to your doctor": name the flagged items from the findings as things worth mentioning, and say hormone therapy or other treatment can be an option to discuss with a GP. Every sentence in "What this may mean" and "Things that may help" ends with its source tag like [S2]; use only the numbered sources provided and never invent a tag. Do not quote figures, lab values or percentages from the sources. If no source fits a point, leave the point out. "What your blood test may show" (only when the reader uploaded a lab report): start from the reader's own values and say whether each is below, within or above the range printed on their report; add what the sources say about that value in the same bullet. A single value is a snapshot and hormones swing during the transition; the cycle day of the draw matters (the material suggests cycle day 2 to 5 for FSH, LH and oestradiol). Combine blood values with the cycle pattern and the reported symptoms only through the computed alignment: when several signals line up you may say the picture is in line with what the material describes for the hormonal transition, as a pattern and never as a diagnosis, and a doctor can check it. For a reader under 40 age makes the transition less likely, not impossible: when the signals line up strongly, say it would be early and a doctor should look at it. Never say the reader's own hormone is low or high; never say a test confirms or rules out anything.

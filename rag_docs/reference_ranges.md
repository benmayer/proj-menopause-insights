## Typical cycle length
Cycle length is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. The NHS describes periods as irregular when the gap between them is shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days. A cycle varying by a few days from month to month is normal. The often-quoted 28 days is an average, not a rule.

## Early transition marker
In the STRAW+10 staging system for reproductive ageing, the early menopausal transition is marked by cycle length becoming more variable: a persistent difference of 7 days or more between consecutive cycle lengths. This is a research staging marker, not a diagnosis, and a single large difference does not count as persistent.

## Late transition marker
In STRAW+10, the late menopausal transition is marked by a gap of 60 days or more without a period (amenorrhea). A gap this long can have other causes, such as pregnancy, stress, illness or medication.

## Limits of these markers
The markers do not apply equally to everyone. Conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome and hypothalamic amenorrhea change cycle patterns, as do hormonal contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding. Studies also show the 7-day marker is not a perfect cut-off. Treat the markers as prompts for a conversation with a clinician.

## When it's suggests seeing a GP
The it's suggested seeing a GP if periods last longer than 7 days, if there is bleeding between periods, if bleeding becomes heavier than before, if periods stop unexpectedly before age 45, or if there is any bleeding after 12 months without a period. Anyone wanting to discuss perimenopause symptoms or options can also book a non-urgent appointment.
